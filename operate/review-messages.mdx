Open **Messages** to see every generated first message, email and LinkedIn, that has not been sent yet. When approval is required, each one waits here until you approve it. Follow-ups never wait here: they are written and sent automatically during the run, whatever the approval setting.

As a member, you see the messages of your own seats. As an admin, you see the whole account. When the queue holds messages from more than one seat, use the **All seats** menu to show one seat.

## Read the queue

The list sits on the left and the open message on the right. On a phone, the message opens over the list. Use the tabs to switch lists:

| Tab | What it lists |
| --- | --- |
| **Needs approval** | Messages waiting for your decision. Shown only while approval is required. |
| **Next to send** | Approved messages. Called **Queued** when approval is off. The count includes only messages the next run will send; a line under the tabs says how many listed messages are not going out on the next run. |
| **Not going out** | Drafts that cannot be sent: the seat is switched off, LinkedIn is off for the seat, LinkedIn is closed for this contact, or there is no deliverable address. |

A badge on a row shows its state:

| Badge | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Sending** | A running pipeline is sending this message. |
| **Held back** | The message is not sent as it stands. See [Held-back messages](#held-back-messages). |
| **Waiting** | The message waits for something first, for example its LinkedIn invite or a reconnected LinkedIn account. The open message says what. |
| **Approve to send** | Approval is off, but this message was written while it was on, so it waits for your approval. |

The open message shows the contact, company, seat, and the date it was generated. A line at the top says what the message was built on, such as a signal from the contact's LinkedIn posts, or that it was written from their role and company. Email messages show the **To** address. Expand **Strategy brief** to see why the message was written.

## Message actions

### Edit

Change the subject or message body directly in the open message. Changes are saved when you press `⌘S` (`Ctrl+S` on Windows), when you approve, or when you move to another message. **Discard** drops unsaved changes. On LinkedIn messages, the subject is used only if LinkedIn delivers the message as an InMail. A message that still holds a placeholder such as `[Name]` or `{{first_name}}` is not saved: **This message still has a placeholder**, followed by the placeholder, tells you to replace it first.

Use editing for a targeted wording correction, such as fixing a name, adjusting the tone, or shortening a sentence, where the underlying approach is sound. The agent never overwrites a message you edited, and an edited message is never held back for age. With approval off, a saved edit is what the next run sends.

### Approve

Select **Approve**, or **Save & approve** when you have unsaved changes, to mark a message ready to send. Approval does not trigger immediate delivery: the message waits for the next pipeline run and the seat's daily limit. The next message in the list opens.

### Regenerate

Discard the current draft and have the agent write a new one on the next message run. To get it sooner, run **Messages** from **Advanced** on **Home**. Choose **Regenerate** when the strategy itself is wrong: the angle is off, the proof point is irrelevant, or the message doesn't fit the persona.

Before you confirm, describe what was wrong in **Optional: what was wrong with it?** Without a reason the agent still tries a different angle; a reason steers it. The agent also steers clear of the angles of the last drafts you replaced, so a later refresh does not bring one back.

**Regenerate** is unavailable when nothing would rewrite the draft, for example when the contact has not been researched yet, the seat has no mailbox connected, or the LinkedIn invite has not gone out. The open message says why.

### Not interesting

Select **Not interesting** to stop outreach to this person. Their outcome becomes **Not interesting** and their drafts leave the queue; the contact and company records stay. Select **All of** the company instead to do the same for everyone there: the company's stage becomes **Not interesting** and the company is added to your blacklist.

To fix a bad draft without stopping the person, use **Regenerate** instead.

<Tip>
  Press `j` and `k` to move through the list, and `⌘↵` to approve the open message.
</Tip>

## Held-back messages

When approval is required and **Hold back stale drafts** is on under **Settings → Schedule**, a message written from a LinkedIn post or another signal more than 30 days ago is held back instead of sent. A message whose signal has ended is held back whatever the approval setting. Messages written from someone's role and company, and messages you edited, are never held back for age.

A banner at the top of **Messages** counts held-back messages. Each one is rewritten from current research on the next message run. To send the copy as it stands, select **Send anyway** in the open message. This approves the copy and restarts its 30-day window.

## LinkedIn messages and the invite

A LinkedIn message is written once the connection request has gone out, and it is sent after the invite, not before. Until then it shows **Waiting**. If the invite is still pending, the message shows the date of the next try. If LinkedIn refuses the send to one person, the message reads **LinkedIn refused this message, so it is tried again on** a date a week later, and the run goes on with the next message. On a seat set to send the LinkedIn invite first, the email waits until the invite is out, for 7 days at most.

## Approval on or off

An admin chooses the delivery mode under **Settings → Schedule → Outreach delivery**: **Approve every message from the dashboard** or **Send everything automatically**. New accounts start with approval on.

With approval on, a seat's run sends the drafts you approved and writes new ones on the same day: the approved drafts it is about to send do not hold the new ones back. Up to one day's worth of drafts waiting for you, the seat's daily limit for the channel, can wait without slowing anything. After that, new drafts and new people slow down, and they stop at two days' worth. The top of **Messages** says so while approval is on: **New drafts pause while two days' worth wait for your approval.** Approve what waits, and the next run writes more. A draft cannot go out in the run that writes it: it waits for your approval first.

With approval off, new messages are approved as they are written. They appear under **Queued**, and you can still edit them before the next run sends them.

When an admin switches approval off, messages still waiting for approval are approved on save. With **Hold back stale drafts** on, those written from a post or signal more than 30 days ago are rewritten first; where nothing can rewrite them yet, they stay held back for you to decide.

## Review the strategy, not only grammar

Open the **Strategy brief** of each message before approving. Confirm all of the following:

- [ ] The person and company details are correct and current
- [ ] The message reflects the prospect's actual role and context
- [ ] Any proof point or case reference is accurate and permitted for use
- [ ] The call to action matches the persona and stage of the relationship
- [ ] Language, formality, and register are appropriate for the recipient
- [ ] The sign-off name and title match the sending representative
- [ ] No claim in the message is unsupported or potentially misleading

<Warning>
  AI-generated text can be factually wrong or contextually inappropriate even when it reads fluently and naturally. Enabling automatic delivery transfers the review responsibility to you — it does not remove it. Treat every message as your own before approving or enabling auto-send.
</Warning>

## After changing the playbook

Existing drafted or approved messages are not automatically regenerated when you update the messaging playbook. New settings take effect only for messages generated after the change.

To apply updated playbook settings to messages that already have a draft:

1. Open **Messages** and pick the affected seat in **All seats** if needed.
2. Select **Regenerate** on each existing draft.
3. Run the pipeline, or **Messages** from **Advanced**, to produce new drafts under the updated settings.
4. Keep approval mode enabled until the updated output looks stable before switching to automatic delivery.

See [Messaging playbook](/configure/messaging-playbook) for the settings that control tone, persona, proof points, and call-to-action for new messages.
