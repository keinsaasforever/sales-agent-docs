The messaging playbook tells the Sales Agent what to say about you. Configure it under **Settings → Prompts**; only admins of your account see this page. How the agent writes — structure, length, and tone rules — is maintained by keinsaas and applies to every message. Each seat's writing style and sign-offs live under **Settings → Account**.

Prompt changes apply to new messages generated after you save; messages already drafted are not rewritten.

## Add success stories

The **Success stories** card holds the real results messages may cite. For each story, enter the company, what you did in one or two sentences, and the result. You can save up to ten, add them in bulk with **Import CSV**, or start from **Download example CSV**.

Each message cites at most one story, and the agent picks the one that fits the prospect best. A story with a concrete number lands hardest, so the dashboard flags a story without a result. With no stories saved, messages are written without proof instead of inventing any.

Stories show as a compact list: one line each with the company, the result, and any persona the story is limited to. Select a line to open and edit it; **Collapse** closes it and **Remove story** deletes it. When you have two or more persona prompts, an open story has an **Only for** row of persona chips. Pick the personas the story proves something to, and the agent uses it only for them. With none picked, the row reads **every persona**.

## Write a prompt for each persona

Select **New Prompt** to describe your company for one persona, or edit an existing one. Each prompt has these fields:

| Field | What to put in it |
|---|---|
| **Persona** | The persona this prompt is for. |
| **Your company & offer** | What you sell and why it is worth a reply: your positioning and the problem you solve. |
| **Who you target & what signals matter** | The pains to look for and which buying signals rank highest. |
| **Persona notes (optional)** | What lands with this persona: emphasis and wording, not strategy. Champions and economic decision-makers usually need different notes. |
| **CTA / offer** | What the message closes on, ideally a value you offer before asking for a meeting. Leave it empty to close on a low-barrier question instead. |

Use these fields to make your audience hooks, language guardrails, and compliance requirements explicit.

By default, keinsaas's rules for structure, length, subject, CTA, and opening win where your notes disagree. Tick **My notes above win over the default writing rules** to let your notes apply for that persona; the prompt then shows **Your rules win**. Either way, the agent never invents a fact, a number, or a customer, and never writes a closing.

The default rules also shape what a message says. It leads with what the reader gains, not with what your product does, uses the prospect's name once, in the greeting, and skips small talk such as "hope you're well". It closes on an ask that costs the reader nothing to answer, such as a yes; anything your offer needs from them comes after they agree. A first LinkedIn message starts short, about three sentences, and runs longer only when a strong signal and rich context carry it. When a formal German (**Sie**) message goes to someone whose last name LinkedIn shows only as an initial, it opens with "Guten Tag," instead of guessing a surname.

A persona marked **Custom** uses a prompt keinsaas wrote for you. Only its CTA can be edited here.

## Go back to an older version

Every saved text is a version. Select the version button on a prompt, such as **v2**, to see all versions of that persona's prompt with their save date. Each one shows how many people a first message of that version reached and how many replied, on either channel, with the reply rate. The numbers count from 6 October 2026, and a version that has not gone out yet shows **No sends yet**. **Live** marks the version in use.

To bring back an older text, select **Restore** next to it. The text opens in the editor as **Restore v…**; select **Save** to make it live again. It is combined with today's writing rules, so keinsaas's current rules stay in force. **Restore** is not offered for a **Custom** prompt.

## Test two prompts against each other

Select the copy icon (**Duplicate as A/B variant**) on a prompt to create a second prompt for the same persona. It opens in the editor as **A/B variant** with a letter: change what you want to test and select **Save**. From then on, new messages for that persona are split evenly across its variants, and the cards show **A/B · 1 in 2** and **Variant A**, **Variant B**. A prospect keeps the variant of their first message, so an email and the LinkedIn message after it come from the same text. Compare the numbers of each variant in its versions. Delete a variant to end the test.

Writing style is not part of the test: a seat's style applies to every variant.

## Configure each seat's voice

Under **Settings → Account**, the **Writing style** card holds **Email — writing style** and **LinkedIn — writing style** for the selected seat. The channels have separate fields because they differ in length, formality, and format. The styles were built from the example messages given during setup; edit them to adjust:

- tone and greeting style
- message length
- level of formality
- sentence rhythm and structure

The seat's owner edits its writing style. Concrete examples teach tone far more effectively than adjectives such as "professional" or "friendly."

## Keep sign-offs and signatures separate

The **How messages end** card on **Settings → Account** sets the closings for the selected seat. The sender adds them after the message is written, so a change applies to every message sent after you save. For a Gmail mailbox, the signature you set here is the only one: the mailbox's own Gmail signature is not added below it.

- **LinkedIn sign-off** — appended to the first LinkedIn message, in the language it is written in. Leave a language empty for no LinkedIn sign-off in that language.
- **Email sign-off** — the closing line above your signature, one per language, so the closing always matches the language of the message body.
- **Email signature** — your contact block, appended under the sign-off.

The Sales Agent assembles each outbound email in a fixed order:

1. Generated message body
2. Language-specific email sign-off
3. Email signature

The **How every email ends** preview shows the result. A warning appears when your sign-offs or signature do not mention the LinkedIn account or mailbox the messages actually send from.

<Warning>
  Do not place a closing phrase such as "Best regards" or "Kind regards" inside your email signature. The configured sign-off is added automatically as a separate step — including one in the signature as well will produce two closings in every message. The preview warns you when your signature starts with a closing line.
</Warning>

Keep cold-email signatures lean. Your name, role, company, and essential contact details are enough. Avoid images and unnecessary links, as they can affect email deliverability.

## Decide how much to review

Under **Settings → Schedule → Outreach delivery**, choose one of two delivery modes. The setting applies to email and LinkedIn messages alike.

| Mode | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Approve every message from the dashboard** | Hold every generated email and LinkedIn message in **Messages** until you approve it. |
| **Send everything automatically** | Make generated messages eligible for automatic sending without a dashboard approval step. |

When you switch from approval to automatic sending, messages still waiting for approval are approved on save. With **Hold back stale drafts** on, the ones written from an old post or signal are rewritten first.

While approval is on, **Hold back stale drafts** keeps a message written from a LinkedIn post or another signal more than 30 days ago from being sent; it is rewritten on the next run instead. Messages built from someone's role and company are never held back.

For a new playbook or after a significant update, require approval for every message until the output quality is consistently correct. Switch to automatic delivery only after you have reviewed a representative sample.

<Note>
  AI-generated messages can contain factual errors, awkward phrasing, or inaccurate claims. You remain responsible for the content sent from your accounts, including when automatic delivery is enabled. Review samples regularly.
</Note>

Once your delivery mode is set, see [Review messages](/operate/review-messages) for the full approval workflow.
