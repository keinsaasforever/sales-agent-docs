Your ideal customer profile (ICP) drives every stage of the Sales Agent — contact search, candidate scoring, and eligibility for automated outreach. Configure everything under **Settings → Targeting**.

## Start with the free-text ICP

Write a concrete description of the combination you actually want to reach, not a broad market category. A focused description produces more relevant scores than a vague one.

Include details such as:

- business model and industry
- company size or growth stage
- geography
- buyer role and seniority
- relevant technologies or commercial signals
- explicit exclusions and poor-fit patterns

**Example:**

> DACH-based food technology companies with fewer than 50 employees and strong recent growth. Target a newly appointed Head of Marketing or Marketing Director located in DACH.

The agent evaluates this free-text description together with the structured criteria below.

## Configure the structured criteria

| Setting | How it is used |
|---|---|
| **Job titles — Tier 1** | Primary roles you want to reach. Use specific decision-maker or champion titles. |
| **Job titles — Tier 2** | Executive fallback roles — such as Founder or Managing Director — used when no Tier 1 contact is available. |
| **Excluded job titles** | Roles that are always skipped, even when they appear in source results. |
| **Locations** | Countries or regions to target. Leave empty for no location filter. |
| **Industries** | Industries to prioritize. Leave empty for no industry filter. |
| **Company size** | Target employee ranges. Contacts at companies with unknown sizes are not automatically rejected. |
| **ICP keywords** | Specialties, business models, or other terms that indicate fit. |
| **Max contacts per company** | Hard cap on the number of people searched at each company. Three to four is a normal starting point; ten is the maximum. |

## Understand the score

Each contact receives a 0–100 ICP score. Three factors contribute to the score, and their relative weights are configurable:

- **Job fit** — how closely the person's role matches your Tier 1 and Tier 2 titles
- **Industry fit** — how well the company's industry aligns with your target list
- **Location fit** — whether the contact is based in a targeted region

Weights are relative, not absolute. A weight setting of `6, 3, 1` means job fit contributes 60%, industry 30%, and location 10%. The dashboard normalizes the values automatically — they do not need to add up to 100.

## Use the two thresholds

### Pipeline ICP threshold

Only contacts at or above this score enter automated outreach. Set it to `0` to allow every contact through regardless of score.

### Phone enrichment threshold

Only contacts at or above this score receive automatic phone lookup. Below the threshold the dashboard prompts for manual confirmation before a lookup runs. Set it to `0` to disable the threshold entirely.

<Warning>
  Do not lower a threshold just to fill the queue. First verify that your source, owner assignment, exclusions, and scoring criteria are correct. A fuller queue built on weaker candidates spends credits and outreach capacity without improving fit quality.
</Warning>

## Refresh existing scores

When you update your criteria, contacts scored under the old settings become outdated. Choose the right refresh method:

- **Recompute** — use this when you change only the score weights. Existing inputs are recalculated without using AI or credits.
- **AI rescore** — use this when you change the meaning of the ICP itself. Existing contacts are re-evaluated against the updated free-text and criteria.

Contacts added after a settings change are always scored against the current configuration automatically.

## Credits and filtering

Credits pay for returned search and enrichment results — not only for the contacts that ultimately enter your pipeline. Candidates that later fail job-title exclusions, ICP requirements, deduplication checks, or company validation still consume the credit that retrieved them.

<Tip>
  When your filters are granular, expect the agent to inspect more candidates than the number it ultimately accepts. Run a small company batch first and review the quality of results before scaling up your search.
</Tip>
