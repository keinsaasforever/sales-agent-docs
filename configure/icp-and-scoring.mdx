Your ideal customer profile (ICP) drives every stage of the Sales Agent — contact search, candidate scoring, and eligibility for automated outreach. Configure everything under **Settings → Targeting**. Only admins of your account see this page. Save the ICP fields with **Save targeting**.

## Start with the free-text ICP

Write a concrete description in **Your ICP in your own words**: the combination you actually want to reach, not a broad market category. A focused description produces more relevant scores than a vague one.

Include details such as:

- business model and industry
- company size or growth stage
- geography
- buyer role and seniority
- relevant technologies or commercial signals
- explicit exclusions and poor-fit patterns

**Example:**

> DACH-based food technology companies with fewer than 50 employees and strong recent growth. Target a newly appointed Head of Marketing or Marketing Director located in DACH.

The AI scores every company and contact against this description together with the structured criteria below.

## Configure the structured criteria

| Setting | How it is used |
|---|---|
| **Job Titles — Tier 1 (primary ICP)** | The roles you actually want. Searched first at every company. |
| **Job Titles — Tier 2 (fallback)** | Roles you would take instead, such as Managing Director at a small company or an adjacent role. Searched only at companies where no Tier 1 title was found. |
| **Seniority Levels** | The levels you want to talk to, from **C-level / Owner** to **Entry / Junior**. Leave everything unticked for no preference. **Decision-makers only** ticks **C-level / Owner** and **VP / Head of / Director** in one click; selling mostly to small companies, add **Manager / Team Lead**, where managers often decide. Selecting it again keeps your selection, and ticking another level leaves the preset. |
| **Excluded Job Titles** | Titles to keep out of outreach, even when they appear in search results. |
| **Locations** | Countries to target. Leave empty for no location filter. |
| **DACH only** | Only people living in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, or Liechtenstein. Overrides **Locations** for people, not for company headquarters, and stops LinkedIn invites to people outside that region. |
| **Industries** | LinkedIn industries to focus on. Leave empty for no industry filter. In the web search for new companies by event they are a preference, not a filter; see [Run signal searches from My jobs](#run-signal-searches-from-my-jobs). |
| **Industries to leave out** | LinkedIn industries the Sales Agent leaves out when it searches for new companies. They also hold for signal searches and are a hard lock there. They do not apply to CSV imports or your own Sales Navigator searches. Use LinkedIn's industry names, such as Banking or Law Practice. The setup wizard asks for the same field. |
| **Company Size** | Headcount ranges to target. Nothing selected means no size filter. Companies whose headcount is unknown are never filtered out. The ranges also feed **Size fit** in the score. |
| **Customers** | Whether the companies you look for sell to businesses or consumers: **Both**, **B2B only**, or **B2C only**. It applies only when you search for new companies in [**Search our database**](/configure/add-prospects#search-at-your-companies). **B2B only** or **B2C only** uses fewer of our search sources, so a search finds fewer people. |
| **ICP Keywords** | Specialties, business models, or other terms that indicate a fitting company. |
| **Max Contacts per Company** | Hard cap per company: the contact search finds at most this many people there, and at most this many are in outreach at once. Three to four is a normal starting point; ten is the maximum. |
| **Fill up to the max** | When the first source finds fewer people than your maximum, the next sources are asked too. Costs about three times the search credits. |
| **Retry bad emails at another source** | When a found email turns out invalid, bounces, or sits on a domain that is not the company's, the contact is looked up again at the next source. Each retry costs extra credits. |

Two rows on the page are collapsed and show their current values: **Scoring** holds the score weights and the two thresholds, and **Search and lookups** holds **Max Contacts per Company**, **Fill up to the max**, **Retry bad emails at another source**, **Queue order**, and **Export LinkedIn posts and comments**. Open a row to change it.

## Contacts outside your filters

**Excluded Job Titles**, **Seniority Levels**, **Locations**, and **Company Size** are hard filters. A contact who fails one is kept but set aside without a seat, so no outreach starts for them. Search results that land there still cost the credits that found them.

- Missing information passes. A contact whose seniority, location, or company size is unknown is never set aside for it.
- A location fails only when neither the person nor the company is in your target locations.
- A title you listed under Tier 1 or Tier 2 wins over **Excluded Job Titles** and **Seniority Levels**.
- Contacts already in outreach are not affected by later filter changes.

On **Prospects**, the **Contacts** tab shows these contacts with the stage **Not in pipeline**. Hover the badge to see the reason, for example `Outside your filters: …`. When you loosen a filter and refresh the scores, contacts that now pass get their seat back.

## Understand the score

Each contact receives a 0–100 **ICP** score. Half of it comes from the person, half from the company. The company's own 0–100 score is called **Company Fit**.

- **Job fit** — how closely the person's title matches your Tier 1 and Tier 2 titles and seniority levels
- **Location fit** — whether the person and the company are in your target locations; it counts in both halves
- **Industry fit** — the company's industry, rated on a built-in industry scale. Your **Industries** list counts through the AI company review instead.
- **Size fit**: how well the company's headcount fits your **Company Size** ranges. It counts only when you have picked sizes and the company's size is known.

An AI review of each company against your ICP then moves the score: up 15 points for a clear fit, down 15 for a poor fit, unchanged when the result is unclear or the company has not been reviewed yet. The review reads the company's description and its own keywords.

Set the factors under **ICP Score Weights**. The weights are relative and do not need to add up to 100. Next to each one, the dashboard shows its real share of the score (**= X% of score**). **Size fit** starts at 0, so it counts only once you raise it. Because the score is split into two halves, the defaults of 50, 30, 20, and 0 make job fit about 36%, industry fit 30%, and location fit 34% of the score. With **Size fit** at 20 and sizes picked, the shares become 36%, 21%, 29%, and 14%.

### Read the score chips

**Prospects → Contacts** and **Prospects → Prospects** show the contact's score in the **ICP** column. **Companies** shows the company's score in **Company Fit**. The contact and prospect panels show both; the company panel shows **Company Fit**. Every score appears as a chip colored by its band:

| Band | Score |
| --- | --- |
| **Low** | below 50 |
| **Medium** | 50 to 69 |
| **High** | 70 to 85 |
| **Excellent** | above 85 |

Tables show the number; hover the chip to see the band. Panels show the number and the band. A **!** next to a score means it is outdated: you changed your targeting after it was calculated (see [Refresh existing scores](#refresh-existing-scores)). A pencil mark means an admin set a company's **Company Fit** by hand; see [Set Company Fit by hand](/operate/prospects-and-companies#set-company-fit-by-hand).

## Use the two thresholds

### Pipeline ICP threshold

Only contacts at or above this score enter automated outreach. Set it to `0` to allow every contact through regardless of score.

### Phone enrichment ICP threshold

Only contacts at or above this score get a phone lookup: in automated outreach runs, in scheduled searching, and in lookups you start yourself. Below the threshold, the dashboard asks you to confirm before a lookup you start runs. Set it to `0` to disable the threshold.

If this threshold is lower than the pipeline threshold, the dashboard warns you: those contacts would get a paid phone lookup but never enter the pipeline. Select **Raise it to** to align the two.

<Warning>
  Do not lower a threshold just to fill the queue. First verify that your source, owner assignment, exclusions, and scoring criteria are correct. A fuller queue built on weaker candidates spends credits and outreach capacity without improving fit quality.
</Warning>

## Choose the queue order

**Queue order** sets the order in which prospects are worked:

- **ICP with company grouping** keeps one company's contacts together, so the account is approached as one conversation. Companies are ranked by their best-scoring contact.
- **ICP only** works contacts strictly by score.

Sorting a table on screen does not change this order.

## Refresh existing scores

Saving **Settings → Targeting** refreshes the scores that code alone can recalculate, at once, without AI or credits:

- Saving a change to weights (**Size fit** included), job titles, seniority levels, excluded titles, company sizes, or locations recalculates the scores of existing contacts and applies your filters to them again. There is nothing to click.
- Saving a change to the free-text ICP, locations, industries, or keywords also asks **Update ICP scores?**. **Update scores now** has the AI re-evaluate your companies in the background, so you can keep working. A status line at the top of every page shows the progress. If the update stops, select **Try again** there. Choose **Later** to skip it.

If the instant recalculation did not finish, the to-do **ICP scores need updating** appears in your notifications with **Recompute scores now**. After you choose **Later**, the to-do stays until you run the AI refresh. Contacts added after a settings change are always scored against the current configuration.

## Set buying signals

Open **Settings → Signal criteria** to define what counts as a reason to write to a company now. Only admins see this page. Companies with a fresh signal are contacted first, and the message builds on the signal. A company's buying intent follows the AI's score of each signal: a strong signal moves it up more than a weak one, and a signal counts less as it ages. This page holds only what counts. Which signal searches run is set on **My jobs**; see [Run signal searches from My jobs](#run-signal-searches-from-my-jobs). The signal searches run only once keinsaas switches them on with you.

- **What you sell**: a few sentences on your offer and who buys it. Every event found is judged against it.
- **Sources**: **News and web** and **Job ads** each have a switch, and both are marked **Used in messages**. What counts here applies to watching your companies, to new companies from signals, and to your messages.
  - **News and web** looks for anything that makes what you sell more urgent. Hints sharpen the search, and none is required. Switch on the standard events **Acquisition**, **New leader**, **Funding**, or **Expansion**, add your own events in your own words (up to 20, each up to 200 characters), or select **Suggest with AI**, which reads **What you sell** and, where it can, your company website, and add the ideas you like with one click; nothing is added until you click. Hover a standard event to see, once there is data, how many first messages that used it were answered in the last 90 days. With nothing set, the search still looks for anything that makes your offer more urgent.
  - **Job ads** looks at open roles. Add the roles or teams that count, or leave it empty for any team you sell to. Your **Tier 1** job titles from **Targeting** are offered as one-click hints.
- **Pages and profiles to watch**: up to 5 LinkedIn company pages or personal profiles whose posts post engagement reads; see [Pages and profiles to watch](#pages-and-profiles-to-watch).
- **Not a signal**: events that should never count, such as awards.
- **Scoring**, a collapsed row: **Minimum signal score**, where a signal scoring below it never counts and never reaches a message; **Use a signal for (days)**, which is how long a signal is used in messages, counted from the day it happened, with half that time for a signal without a date; and **Which signals each persona hears about**. The persona chips decide which signals a message to that persona may use. The search is the same for every persona. Untick them all for people who should get no signal at all.

Save the page with **Save signals**. Companies with signals show a [**Why now**](/operate/prospects-and-companies#why-now) section in their panels and a **Signals** tab on prospects, where everyone can [rate each signal](/operate/prospects-and-companies#rate-a-signal). The **Buying intent** and **Intent** columns appear.

### Pages and profiles to watch

**Post engagement** reads the recent posts of your own company page and of up to 5 LinkedIn company pages or personal profiles, such as a competitor or a founder your buyers follow, and brings in up to 20 new people a day. Paste each as a link such as `linkedin.com/company/name` or `linkedin.com/in/name` and press `Enter`; anything else reads **That is not a LinkedIn company page or profile.** People who comment on or react to those posts come in with their company, and someone already on your list becomes a signal there instead. The person you watch and their colleagues, like a watched company's own staff, are not brought in. See [Followers and engagement](#followers-and-engagement).

## Run signal searches from My jobs

**My jobs** holds the switches for the signal jobs, and **Signal criteria** holds what counts. Which cards you see depends on what keinsaas has switched on with you.

### Watch your companies

**Watch your companies** checks your best-fit companies that have no live signal, each again after the number of days set with you, for 1 credit per company checked. The card shows the last check, the companies up next, and how many companies had a signal in the last 30 days. keinsaas switches the checks on with you; where it gave you the switch, the card shows it. Signals it finds put those companies first in your automatic search.

### New companies from signals

**New companies from signals** finds companies that match your **Targeting** where something just happened, such as funding or a new leader. Its signals come with the automatic search **New companies and their people**, so the card shows only while that is your automatic search; see [New companies and their people](/configure/add-prospects#new-companies-and-their-people). People at these companies take the first places in your **New people a day**, up to **Max Contacts per Company** each.

The **At a glance** box on the card and in the search's step 3 shows what runs every weekday and each source's limit:

- **News and events**: a weekly web search, 2 credits per new company. The weekly cap follows your **New people a day**: five times that number divided by **Max Contacts per Company**, at least 5 and at most 100 new companies a week.
- **Job ads**: companies hiring for the roles in **Signal criteria**, from a public job board in Germany only, at most 30 companies a night, 0.25 credits per company looked up.
- **Sales Navigator spotlights**: free.
- **Page followers** and **Post engagement**: free; see [Followers and engagement](#followers-and-engagement).

Every signal is rated for quality: a strong one moves the person up your queue, a weak one doesn't count. Admins flip the card's switch between signals first (**On**) and your Targeting alone (**Off**). With **Only companies with a signal**, the card has no switch: change it with **Edit**, whose last step shows the price before you save. **Run now** starts news and job ads at once, within the week's news cap. It is greyed out with its reason while a search runs, the cap is used up, or no source is on. While your automatic search is off, the card reads **News and job ads wait until your automatic search is on.**

### Followers and engagement

**Followers and engagement** brings in people who show interest on LinkedIn, with their company. **Page followers** reads who follows your company page; someone already on your list, or at a company on it, becomes a signal there instead of a new lead. **Comments and reactions on posts** reads the recent posts of your company page and of the [pages and profiles to watch](#pages-and-profiles-to-watch), up to 20 new people a day; someone already on your list becomes a signal there. Both are read in the daily run of a seat with Sales Navigator and are free; their emails and phones are charged when they enter outreach. Where signals are not switched on for you, this is its own card on **My jobs**, and admins switch each one; otherwise you tick them in the automatic search. A follow counts toward buying intent and the **Buying signals** filter, but it only moves the company up the list: a message never mentions it.

**Industries** from **Targeting** are a preference for the web search by event: it looks for companies working in or near them, neighbouring industries count, and a company outside them scores lower instead of being dropped. **Industries to leave out** stay a hard lock: a company in one is left out.

### The market rule

Where **New companies from signals** is switched on, a line at the top of **My jobs** and **Add to Pipeline** says whether to start from signals or to work through your companies and watch them. It compares the companies that match your **Targeting** with what your seats reach in about three months: the new people they contact each working day, over 65 working days, divided by the people per company on **Targeting**. **Details** shows the count, the reach, and about how many credits a month each way costs. For admins, the matching choices carry **Recommended**: in the search's step 1 **People at my companies** or **New companies and their people**, and its **Automatically, every weekday**; in step 3 **Signals first, then Targeting** or **Only companies with a signal**; on **My jobs** the card of **Watch your companies** or, while it is off, of **New companies from signals**. When a public list fits your Targeting, **Build a company list** carries the step 1 tag instead; see [Search at your companies](/configure/add-prospects#search-at-your-companies). **Job ads** carries **Recommended** where **Signal criteria** hold roles and your Targeting reaches Germany. It is advice, not a lock.

When the count is missing, or **Targeting** changed since it was made, admins select **Count my market**. It is free when a seat with Sales Navigator reads it in its next daily run; otherwise it costs up to 0.25 credits.

## Other targeting settings

The first setting below sits in the **Search and lookups** row; the last two sit in the **Phones and calls** card.

- **Export LinkedIn posts and comments** — when on, the agent reads what each prospect recently posted or commented on LinkedIn before writing to them. It takes a few minutes per prospect. Turning it off also disables the **Settings → Schedule** rule **No posts or comments in the last 30 days**.
- **Phone lookups per seat** — whether each seat buys phone numbers at all, for contacts at or above the phone enrichment threshold. Email is always looked up. A lookup you start yourself runs regardless, and credits are charged only when a number is found.
- **Call list** — **Show a lead on the To Call tab** decides how early a lead with a phone number appears there, from **When the invite is sent** to **When all follow-ups are done (default)**, or **Never**. A lead you can no longer reach any other way always appears. This setting never changes what the agent sends. The default, **When all follow-ups are done**, waits until no channel has anything left to send. A LinkedIn invitation with no answer after 30 days no longer holds a lead back, so the lead joins **To Call** and the **Follow-ups done** tab.

Automatic imports and searches live on **Add to Pipeline**, not here: see [Add prospects](/configure/add-prospects).

## Credits and filtering

Credits pay for returned search and enrichment results — not only for the contacts that ultimately enter your pipeline. Candidates that later fall outside your filters, fail ICP requirements, or are removed as duplicates still consume the credit that retrieved them.

<Tip>
  When your filters are granular, expect the agent to inspect more candidates than the number it ultimately accepts. Run a small company batch first and review the quality of results before scaling up your search.
</Tip>
